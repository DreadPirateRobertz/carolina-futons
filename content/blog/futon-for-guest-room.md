A spare room is one of the best problems to have. A home office, a craft room, a gym corner — and when guests arrive, a bedroom. The challenge is making it work as all of those things at once.

A futon is the piece that makes it possible. Here's how to turn a spare room into a guest suite that actually feels comfortable, inviting, and like a real bedroom — not a folding cot pushed against a wall.

## Why a Futon Works Better Than a Daybed or Sofa Bed

There are three common approaches to spare room sleeping: a daybed, a sofa bed, and a futon. Each works, but futons hit a sweet spot that the others miss.

**Daybeds** look beautiful and provide a true bed position, but most daybeds are twin-size — too narrow for couples. They also take up permanent floor space whether you're using them as a bed or not.

**Sofa beds** fold flat and hide inside a couch frame. The problem is the fold-out mechanism leaves a metal crossbar under the mattress that guests feel all night. And the mattress is thin — usually 4-5 inches — designed to fold, not to sleep.

**Futons** combine a real sleeping surface (8-10 inches of foam or innerspring) with full flexibility. In sofa position, the room works as an office or sitting area. One motion converts it to a full or queen bed. The mattress is the sleeping surface — no bars, no thin padding, just the same mattress your guests would sleep on in any other bedroom.

## Choosing the Right Futon for a Guest Room

Not all futons are equally suited to guest duty. Here's what to look for:

### Frame: Go with Hardwood

Solid hardwood frames — ash, rubberwood, or poplar — look like real furniture and hold up to repeated conversion cycles. The Night & Day Furniture Montage frame in cherry or chocolate finish blends into any room style. It doesn't look like a futon. It looks like a sofa.

Wall-hugger frames are especially useful in spare rooms. They convert from sofa to bed without needing extra clearance in front — the frame tilts back rather than forward. If space is tight, a wall-hugger lets you place the futon closer to a desk or bookshelf without rearranging the room when guests arrive.

### Mattress: 8 Inches Minimum

Guest comfort lives or dies with the mattress. Don't skimp here. An 8-inch Otis Bed foam mattress provides real sleeping support — the kind that lets guests wake up without back pain and without politely telling you they slept fine when they didn't.

If guests stay more than two nights, look at a 10-inch innerspring mattress. Innerspring construction provides better edge support and breathability for extended stays.

### Cover: Choose It Like Bedroom Bedding

The cover is what guests see and feel. Choose it the way you'd choose a bedroom comforter — not as an afterthought. A solid linen-look cover in a neutral tone (sand, charcoal, slate blue) makes the futon look intentional. It reads as a sofa when it's upright and disappears when you add regular pillows and a throw blanket.

Cotton duck covers hold up through washing and look better over time. Microfiber covers are easier to clean but start looking worn after a year of guest use.

## Styling the Room to Feel Like a Guest Suite

The furniture is one piece. The room styling is the rest.

### Layer Bedding Like a Real Bedroom

When guests arrive, make up the futon like a bed: fitted sheet, flat sheet, duvet or comforter, pillowcases on real bed pillows. The bolster pillows that prop against the wall in sofa mode go to the floor at night. A good duvet cover transforms a futon from "couch I'm sleeping on" to "bed I was given."

Guests notice the difference. A futon made up properly feels like a guest room, not an improvised couch situation.

### Give Them Surface Space

A spare room guest suite needs somewhere to put things: phone, water glass, reading material, glasses case. A small side table or nightstand next to the futon solves this. If there's no room, a wall-mounted shelf works equally well.

Guests who have nowhere to put their phone end up leaving it on the floor. Small detail, but it signals whether the room was set up for them or just converted hastily.

### Clear a Drawer or Section of Closet

Even for a two-night stay, guests appreciate somewhere to unpack. If the room doubles as a home office, clear one desk drawer before they arrive. If there's a closet, move your seasonal storage to one side and leave a section with hangers free.

### Lighting Matters More Than You Think

Home offices are lit for working — bright overhead light, maybe a task lamp. Guest bedrooms need softer, warmer light for winding down. A small bedside lamp with a warm bulb (2700-3000K) changes the feel of the room completely. It doesn't require rewiring — a simple plug-in lamp on the side table solves it.

## The Conversion Routine

Part of making a futon guest room work is making the conversion seamless for you and clear for guests.

**Before guests arrive:** Convert to bed mode, make it up with full bedding. Have the room ready when they walk in.

**For extended stays:** Leave the futon in bed mode. Use the room as-is, which may mean rearranging the desk or work area temporarily.

**After guests leave:** Strip the bedding, air out the mattress for a few hours if possible, vacuum the cover, and convert back to sofa mode. The room returns to normal use.

## FAQ

**What size futon is best for a guest room?**

Full size works for single guests or couples who don't mind close quarters. Queen is the better choice if you regularly host couples or guests who prefer more sleeping space. Most spare rooms fit a queen futon comfortably — they're narrower than a traditional queen bed frame because there's no headboard and minimal frame width.

**Can guests sleep on a futon comfortably for several nights?**

Yes, with the right mattress. An 8-inch or thicker foam or innerspring futon mattress provides the same support as a standard guest mattress. Thin futon mattresses (6 inches or under) aren't suitable for multi-night stays. Our Otis Bed 8-inch Harmony or 10-inch Spring Coil are designed for nightly use.

**Do I need special sheets for a futon?**

Standard fitted sheets work on futon mattresses. Full-size futon mattresses use full-size sheets. Queen futon mattresses use queen sheets. The mattress depth is similar to a standard mattress, so deep-pocket sheets aren't necessary unless your mattress is unusually thick.

**How do I keep the futon mattress from sliding during conversion?**

Use a non-slip mattress pad between the mattress and the frame. Most futon frames also have small lip edges on the slats that hold the mattress in position during conversion. If the mattress slides in your specific frame, a rubberized pad under the mattress solves it in seconds.

**Is a futon or a daybed better for a guest room?**

For a single guest, both work. For couples or for guests who prefer more space, a full or queen futon is better — daybeds are typically twin-sized. Futons also convert to sofa mode more flexibly than daybeds, which maintain a single fixed profile.
