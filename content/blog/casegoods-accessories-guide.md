"Casegoods" is an industry term for case-style furniture — pieces built around a box or case structure, like chests, dressers, bookcases, cabinets, and storage ottomans. Combined with the right accessories, casegoods are what transform a collection of furniture pieces into a cohesive, finished room.

This guide covers how to choose casegoods and accessories that work with your existing furniture, fit your space, and hold up over time.

## What Are Casegoods? The Furniture Category Explained

In retail furniture, "casegoods" refers to all non-upholstered furniture pieces that provide structure and storage. Examples include:

- **Accent tables** — end tables, side tables, sofa tables/consoles
- **Storage ottomans** — upholstered pieces with hinged tops
- **Bookcases & shelving units** — freestanding or modular
- **Media consoles** — low-profile TV stands and media cabinets
- **Accent cabinets** — side cabinets, drink cabinets, decorative storage
- **Room accessories** — pillows, throws, lamp bases, trays, decorative objects

Casegoods are the supporting cast of a room — they don't usually anchor the space the way a sofa or bed does, but without them, the room feels unfinished.

## Why Casegoods and Accessories Matter

Imagine a room with a beautiful futon sofa, a hardwood coffee table, and nothing else. No end tables. No lamp. No throw blanket. No pillows. The room is "furnished" but feels empty and cold.

Now add: a wood-and-metal end table with a ceramic lamp, a linen throw folded over one arm of the futon, two contrast-color accent pillows, and a small tray on the coffee table. Same room. Completely different feeling.

Casegoods and accessories are what convert floor space into livable, layered space. The return on investment is disproportionately high: a $150 end table and a $40 lamp can do more for a room's appearance than a $300 upgrade on the sofa.

## Choosing Accent Tables: End Tables, Side Tables, and Sofa Tables

### End Tables

End tables sit beside seating — typically at the ends of a sofa or futon. They hold lamps, drinks, books, and remotes.

**Height:** Should be within 2 inches of the sofa arm height. If your futon arm is 24 inches tall, look for end tables in the 22–26 inch range.

**Style match:** End tables don't need to perfectly match your futon or coffee table. A metal-and-wood end table pairs well with a wood futon frame. A painted or lacquered end table works with modern or eclectic rooms.

**Size:** The tabletop should be large enough to hold a standard lamp base (10–12" diameter) plus a coaster and a book. Most end tables in the 18–24" square range work well.

### Sofa Tables / Console Tables

A sofa table is a narrow, tall table placed directly behind a sofa or futon when the piece is positioned away from the wall. It provides a surface for lamps, plants, and decorative objects — and visually "anchors" the sofa from the rear.

**Ideal height:** 2–4 inches taller than the sofa back. For a 30" sofa back, look for a 32–34" console.

**Ideal depth:** 10–18 inches. Just deep enough for a lamp base and a few objects. Anything deeper starts to feel like a separate piece of furniture, not a console.

### Coffee Tables

The coffee table is the center of gravity of a living room. Rule of thumb: **the coffee table should be about two-thirds the length of the sofa.**

- 72" sofa → 48" coffee table
- 84" futon → 54–60" coffee table

**Height:** Standard sofa seat height is 17–19 inches. Coffee table height should be within 4 inches of the seat height (lower is generally preferred for comfort).

## Storage Ottomans: The Most Flexible Casegood

A storage ottoman is one of the most versatile furniture accessories available. It serves as:

- **Footrest** when you're sitting on the sofa
- **Extra seating** for guests (typically rated for 200–300 lbs)
- **Coffee table substitute** (add a serving tray on top for a stable surface)
- **Storage unit** (blankets, pillows, games, remote controls)

**Size for futon use:** A round ottoman (24–28" diameter) or rectangular ottoman (20" × 40") works well with a standard full or queen futon.

**Upholstery:** Choose something cleanable. Faux leather or microfiber are the practical choices. Velvet looks beautiful but shows wear faster.

## Accent Pillows: The Quickest Room Refresh

Accent pillows on a futon or sofa accomplish two things: they add color and pattern interest, and they signal "someone actually thought about this room."

**The 3-pillow rule for futons:**
- 2 identical pillows at each end of the futon (12–20" square or rectangular)
- 1 contrasting or patterned pillow in the center

**Color strategy:**
- If your futon cover is a solid neutral, use one bold accent color in the pillows plus one pattern
- If your futon cover has pattern or texture, keep pillows in solid complementary colors
- Limit accent colors to 2–3 maximum for a cohesive look

**Pillow inserts:** Always buy inserts rated for the pillow cover size or one size up. A 20" cover with a 20" insert will look plump and full. A 20" cover with an 18" insert looks deflated.

## Throws and Blankets: Function and Finishing Touch

A folded throw blanket on the arm or back corner of a futon is one of the simplest styling moves there is. It adds texture, warmth (literally and visually), and a casual, lived-in quality that makes a space feel welcoming.

**Choose by texture:**
- **Chunky knit or woven cotton:** Casual, farmhouse, or mountain aesthetic
- **Sherpa or faux fur:** Cozy and inviting — works in any informal space
- **Linen or lightweight cotton:** Clean and modern — better for contemporary or coastal styles

**Color:** If your room's palette is neutral (grays, whites, tans), this is a safe place to introduce a single pop of color. A terra cotta or forest green throw on a charcoal futon adds warmth without overwhelming the room.

## Lamps: The Most Overlooked Accessory

Overhead lighting makes a room functional. Table and floor lamps make it livable. The single most common mistake in furnished rooms is relying entirely on ceiling lights — which cast harsh, flat illumination from above — when adding a table lamp creates warm, directional light that makes the entire room feel more comfortable.

**For a futon + living area:**
- 1 floor lamp behind or beside the futon (great for reading)
- 1–2 end table lamps (matching or complementary pair)

**Wattage:** LED bulbs at 800–1100 lumens (60–75W equivalent) in a warm white color temperature (2700–3000K) create the right living room ambiance.

## How to Shop Casegoods: A Quick Checklist

Before buying any casegood or accessory:

- [ ] **Measure the space.** Note the exact available footprint and ceiling height if relevant.
- [ ] **Check the finish against your existing pieces.** Perfect matching is unnecessary (and can look rigid), but finishes should feel related — all warm tones or all cool tones.
- [ ] **Consider weight and mobility.** If you frequently rearrange furniture, avoid pieces that require two people to move.
- [ ] **Verify material quality.** Solid wood and metal beat particleboard and hollow core for long-term durability.
- [ ] **Think about the season.** Heavy-textured accessories (chunky throws, dark velvet pillows) feel mismatched in summer rooms. Lighter textures and cooler colors work year-round.

## Casegoods & Accessories FAQs

**Q: Do all my furniture pieces need to match?**
A: No — and in most well-designed rooms, they don't. A mix of materials and styles looks more collected and personal than a perfectly matched set. The rule is: pieces should feel *related*, not identical. Sharing a wood tone or metal finish is usually enough cohesion.

**Q: What's the best way to add storage to a small living room?**
A: Vertical. A tall bookcase or shelving unit (72"+) provides substantial storage without consuming much floor space. Add a storage ottoman for a second layer of discreet storage under a horizontal surface you're already using.

**Q: How many accent pillows is too many?**
A: For a standard full or queen futon: 4–5 pillows maximum. Beyond that, you're constantly moving pillows to actually sit down, which defeats the purpose. A 2+1 arrangement (2 anchors, 1 accent in center) is the practical sweet spot.

**Q: Can I use regular bedroom nightstands as futon end tables?**
A: Often yes — a standard nightstand at 24–28" height works as an end table beside a futon with a 24–28" arm height. Just verify the height relationship before purchasing.
