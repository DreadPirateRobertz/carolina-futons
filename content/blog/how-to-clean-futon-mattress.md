# How to Clean Your Futon Mattress (Without Ruining It)

**Target keywords:** how to clean futon mattress, clean futon mattress, futon mattress cleaning, remove stains futon mattress
**Meta description:** Learn how to clean a futon mattress safely — from spot cleaning spills to deep-cleaning stains and deodorizing. Step-by-step guide that won't damage the foam or fabric.

---

A futon mattress gets more contact than most household surfaces. It's a sofa by day and a bed by night. Spills happen. Odors accumulate. Over time, even a well-maintained mattress needs more than a quick vacuum.

The challenge is that futon mattresses — whether foam, innerspring, or cotton — can be damaged by the wrong cleaning methods. Too much water saturates foam and promotes mold. Harsh chemicals break down fabric. Heat from a dryer ruins the internal structure.

Here's exactly how to clean a futon mattress the right way, from quick spill response to deep deodorizing — without damaging what you paid for.

## What's Inside Your Futon Mattress

Cleaning approach depends on what's inside. Most modern futon mattresses fall into one of three categories:

**Foam-filled:** High-density polyurethane foam, sometimes with a cotton quilting layer on top. The most common type. Foam does not tolerate soaking — moisture gets trapped inside and won't dry, leading to mold and breakdown of the foam structure.

**Innerspring:** Metal coil core with foam padding and fabric cover. Similar care requirements to foam — avoid saturating. The coils can rust if the mattress stays wet.

**Cotton or cotton-blend:** Traditional Japanese-style futon construction, or transitional mattresses with significant cotton fill. These are actually the most washable, but still shouldn't be machine-washed whole. Cotton fill can bunch and lose its shape.

## Routine Cleaning: What to Do Weekly and Monthly

Most mattress cleaning happens before you have a specific stain. Routine care prevents buildup and extends the life of your mattress considerably.

### Weekly: Vacuum

Use an upholstery attachment on a regular vacuum. Run it across the entire surface — top, sides, and if accessible, the bottom. This removes dust, allergens, pet dander, and small particles that work into the fabric and accelerate wear.

Pay attention to seams and the fold crease. The fold crease is where the mattress bends during conversion, and debris concentrates there over time.

### Monthly: Air Out

Remove the futon cover (if it's a separate slipcover) or prop the mattress upright near a window on a dry day. Leave it for 4-6 hours. UV light and airflow kill bacteria and moisture that accumulates from body heat and humidity.

This is especially important in humid climates. In western North Carolina, where we're based, a futon mattress that isn't aired out regularly can develop a musty smell over months even without visible stains.

### Every 3-6 Months: Rotate

Futon mattresses wear unevenly because the same areas — where hips and shoulders press — take the most compression. Rotating head-to-foot every few months distributes this wear. Otis Bed mattresses are no-flip, so rotation is all that's needed.

## Spot Cleaning: Handling Fresh Spills

Act within the first few minutes of a spill. Fresh spills are dramatically easier to remove than dried-in stains.

### Step-by-Step Spot Cleaning

**1. Blot immediately — do not rub.** Rubbing spreads the liquid deeper into the mattress and pushes it into the foam. Use a clean white cloth or paper towels, pressing firmly and lifting straight up. Repeat with fresh cloth until no more liquid transfers.

**2. Mix your cleaning solution.** One teaspoon of mild dish soap (Dawn or similar) in two cups of cool water. Do not use hot water — heat sets protein-based stains like blood or food. Do not use bleach on colored fabric covers.

**3. Apply with a cloth, not by pouring.** Dip a clean cloth in the solution and wring it nearly dry. Dab onto the stain. You want the surface damp, not wet. The goal is surface cleaning, not soaking the foam underneath.

**4. Work from the outside in.** Start at the edge of the stain and work toward the center. This prevents spreading the stain into clean areas.

**5. Rinse with plain water.** Dip a clean cloth in plain cool water, wring nearly dry, and dab over the area to remove soap residue. Soap left in the mattress attracts more dirt.

**6. Dry thoroughly.** Blot with a dry cloth to absorb remaining moisture. Then air-dry — position the mattress near airflow or a fan. Do not put it back in use until completely dry, which may take several hours.

## Removing Specific Stains

Different stains respond to different approaches.

### Urine

Act immediately. Blot as much moisture as possible. Mix one cup white vinegar, one cup water, and two tablespoons of baking soda. Apply with a damp cloth. Let sit for 10 minutes, then blot. The vinegar neutralizes the ammonia in urine. Follow with a baking soda application (see deodorizing section below) to absorb remaining odor.

For dried urine stains: an enzymatic cleaner (available at pet stores) is the most effective option. Enzymes break down the urine proteins that cause both the stain and the odor.

### Coffee and Tea

These are tannin stains — they respond to salt and cold water when fresh. Immediately sprinkle salt on the stain and let it absorb for one minute before blotting. Then clean with the dish soap solution. For dried coffee stains, a mix of white vinegar and dish soap applied with a damp cloth usually lifts them within a few applications.

### Blood

Use cold water only — hot water permanently sets blood. Blot with cold water, then apply hydrogen peroxide (3%, the kind from the pharmacy) with a cloth. The peroxide bubbles on contact, lifting the stain. Blot thoroughly and rinse with cold water. Do not leave hydrogen peroxide on dark fabrics too long — it can lighten color.

### Food and Grease

Scrape off any solid debris first. Apply a small amount of undiluted dish soap directly to the grease stain, let sit for two minutes, then clean with the standard dish soap and water method. Dish soap is designed to cut grease — it works on fabric the same way it works on dishes.

### Sweat and Body Oils

These build up gradually and cause yellowing and odor over time. A baking soda treatment (described below) handles the odor. For visible yellowing, a mixture of hydrogen peroxide and dish soap left on the stain for 15 minutes before blotting often lightens it significantly.

## Deep Deodorizing with Baking Soda

Baking soda is the most effective odor treatment for futon mattresses, and it won't damage foam, fabric, or stitching.

**Step-by-step:**

1. Strip the mattress of any cover or bedding.
2. Sprinkle a generous, even layer of dry baking soda over the entire surface.
3. Let it sit for a minimum of 30 minutes — up to several hours for strong odors. The baking soda absorbs volatile compounds from sweat, body oils, and general use odors.
4. Vacuum thoroughly to remove all baking soda.
5. Flip or rotate, and repeat on the other side.

For a heavily odored mattress, repeat this process twice. Adding a few drops of essential oil (lavender or tea tree) to the baking soda before applying gives a light fresh scent, though it isn't necessary.

## What Not to Do

These mistakes damage mattresses quickly:

**Don't soak.** Soaking foam causes mold inside the mattress that you can't see or reach. Even if the surface dries, the interior stays wet.

**Don't use a steam cleaner.** The heat damages foam structure and drives moisture deep into the mattress.

**Don't machine wash a futon mattress.** It won't survive the wash cycle, and the foam will break apart.

**Don't use harsh chemicals like bleach, acetone, or industrial cleaners.** These degrade foam and fabric simultaneously.

**Don't dry in direct sun for extended periods.** A few hours of indirect sunlight is beneficial. Hours of direct sun degrades foam and fades fabric.

## When to Replace Instead of Clean

Some damage is permanent. If your mattress has visible mold growth (black or green spots, musty smell that persists through cleaning), structural breakdown (foam crumbling, indentations that don't recover), or persistent allergen issues despite thorough cleaning — it's time to replace, not clean.

## FAQ

**Can I put a futon mattress in the washing machine?**

No. A full futon mattress cannot be machine washed — the foam or spring interior will be destroyed by the spin cycle and won't dry properly. Futon slipcovers (the removable fabric cover) are machine washable on cold. Always check the cover's care tag first.

**How do I get the smell out of a futon mattress?**

Baking soda is the most effective deodorizer. Sprinkle it generously, let sit for 30 minutes to several hours, then vacuum thoroughly. For urine or organic odors, use an enzymatic cleaner first, then follow with baking soda. Regular airing out prevents odor buildup.

**How do I clean a futon mattress that got rained on or wet?**

Act fast. Blot as much water as possible with dry towels. Stand the mattress upright in a warm, well-ventilated area with a fan running. Check for complete dryness after 24-48 hours before using. If the mattress smells musty after drying, it has mold inside and should be replaced.

**How often should I clean my futon mattress?**

Vacuum weekly or bi-weekly. Air out monthly. Spot clean immediately after any spill. Deep deodorize with baking soda every 3-6 months or whenever you notice odor buildup. Following this schedule, a quality futon mattress stays fresh for a decade or more.

**Can I use fabric freshener spray on a futon mattress?**

Fabric fresheners like Febreze mask odor temporarily but don't remove the source. For light freshness maintenance between deeper cleanings, they're fine. For actual odor issues — body oils, spills, pet accidents — use baking soda or an enzymatic cleaner instead. Fabric fresheners also leave residue that builds up over time.
