Materials 101

Why Wood Matters:
Rubberwood vs MDF vs Metal

The frame material determines how your futon looks, sounds, feels, and lasts. Here's the honest breakdown — including what we don't sell and why.

The Contenders

Our Pick

Plantation-Grown Rubberwood (Night & Day Furniture)

  • Strength: Comparable to oak. Rated for 500+ lbs.
  • Sustainability: Plantation-grown in Malaysia from rubber trees at end of latex production. Not rainforest wood.
  • Joinery: Mortise-and-tenon joints. The same technique used in fine cabinetry.
  • Noise: Quiet. Wood-to-wood joints don't squeak like metal.
  • Finishes: Takes stain beautifully. Cherry, Dark Chocolate, Natural, Rosewood.
  • Lifespan: 15-25 years. The frame will outlast multiple mattresses.
  • Drawback: Heavier. A Queen frame weighs 80-120 lbs. This is actually a good sign.
Also Great

Virginia Tulip Poplar (KD Frames)

  • Strength: Kiln-dried hardwood. 600 lb weight capacity.
  • Sustainability: Harvested from sustainably managed Virginia forests.
  • Made in USA: Manufactured in Athens, Georgia.
  • Finish: Unfinished — you can stain, paint, or leave natural.
  • Best for: Platform beds and minimalist frames.
  • Lifespan: 20+ years.
Avoid

Particleboard / MDF

  • What it is: Wood chips or fibers bonded with resin and pressed into sheets.
  • Problem: Doesn't hold screws well over time. Swells with humidity. Cracks at stress points.
  • Noise: Creaks as joints loosen.
  • Lifespan: 2-5 years with regular use.
  • Where you find it: Most sub-$300 futon frames online.
  • We don't sell it. Life's too short for furniture that gives up.
Situational

Metal Frames

  • Pros: Cheap. Light. Easy to move.
  • Cons: Squeaks. Cold to touch. Limited style. Welds can fatigue.
  • Best for: Very occasional use. Temporary housing.
  • Not ideal for: Nightly sleeping. Metal frame joints flex under repeated load.
  • We carry a few for specific use cases, but they're not our recommendation for anything long-term.

Mountain Furniture Nerd Fact

Rubberwood trees produce latex for ~25 years. When latex production declines, the trees are harvested for furniture wood and new trees are planted. So every Night & Day frame is literally made from a tree that already had a full career in rubber production. Talk about a second act.
