Inside an Otis Bed: What Makes a Mattress Last 15 Years

Most futon mattresses are an afterthought. Otis Bed mattresses are engineered. Here's what's actually inside — and why it matters for your back, your wallet, and your sleep.

The Layers (Outside In)

1

Quilted Cotton Cover

Breathable cotton ticking quilted to a thin foam layer. Handles stitched in for easy flipping. Machine-assembled with reinforced seams.

2

Comfort Foam Layer

1-2" of CertiPUR-US certified polyurethane foam. This is the layer you feel first — soft enough to contour, firm enough to support. Density: 1.8+ lb/ft³ (cheap mattresses use 1.0-1.2).

3

Innerspring Core (Haley & Trundle models)

Bonnell coil springs sandwiched between foam layers. This is what gives you pushback and prevents the "hammock effect." The springs distribute weight across the entire surface — no pressure points.

4

High-Density Support Base

2" of firm high-density foam at the bottom. Prevents you from feeling the slats and gives the mattress its structural integrity for folding.

Why "8 Inches" Is the Magic Number

At 6 inches, your body compresses through the comfort layer and hits the support base within the first month. You start feeling the frame beneath you. At 8 inches, the foam and spring layers have enough room to do their job for years. The compression curve stays comfortable instead of bottoming out.

This is why we're obsessed with true thickness. Some brands measure at the seam (where it's thickest) and use 6" of actual foam. Otis Bed measures at the sleep surface. 8" means 8".
